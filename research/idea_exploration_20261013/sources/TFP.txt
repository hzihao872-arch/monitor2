0116: However, many VLA policies remain largely reactive: each action is predicted
0117: from the current observation, instruction, and proprioceptive state. This is
0118: insufficient for stage-dependent manipulation, where the same visible scene may
0119: require different actions depending on latent task progress and previous
0120: interaction outcomes. For example, in object swapping, a visually similar state
0121: may correspond to moving the first object to a buffer, moving the second object
0122: into the first object’s original location, or terminating the task.
0123: This motivates adding memory, or task belief, to VLA policies. We use
0124: “belief” to denote a learned latent memory state that summarizes hidden task
0125: progress relevant to future actions. However, robotic manipulation requires more than storing past observations. A useful belief must also decide
0126: when
0127: to change. During
0128: stable transport or occlusion, the policy should preserve task-progress
0129: information despite ambiguous visual evidence; near contact, release, or
0130: subgoal completion, it should rapidly incorporate new evidence. Thus, the key
0131: question is not only whether a VLA has memory, but whether its memory update
0132: follows the event structure and temporal dynamics of manipulation.
0133: Existing memory-aware robot policies mainly either retrieve from history buffers
0134: or maintain recurrent latent states

0146: . History-based methods preserve rich episodic
0147: detail but do not explicitly form a compact task-progress state; recurrent
0148: methods provide such a state, but updates are typically indexed by policy updates rather than manipulation events and elapsed
0149: physical time.
0150: We argue that chunked visuomotor control calls for a dynamics-aware belief
0151: update. A practical controller may re-query earlier when a chunk becomes unreliable, unstable, or
0152: requires correction after contact. Thus, the interval between policy queries is
0153: a physical-time variable, not merely a discrete step index.
0154: Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) networks provide a natural mechanism for such belief
0155: tracking. Their hidden states evolve through input-dependent time constants,
0156: allowing the model to retain information when task context should persist and to
0157: update more rapidly when new evidence appears

0160: ,

0163: .
0164: This continuous-time inductive bias is well matched to memory-dependent
0165: manipulation, where completed subgoals, prior contacts, and interaction outcomes
0166: may remain relevant across multiple action chunks.
0167: We introduce
0168: Temporally Conditioned Memory-Fusion Policies (TFP)
0169: , a
0170: belief-conditioned VLA framework for robotic manipulation. TFP maintains an
0171: episode-local latent belief with LTC dynamics: the previous hidden state carries
0172: retained task context, the current visual and proprioceptive observation proposes
0173: an update, and elapsed time controls how strongly the belief is retained or
0174: revised. The updated belief is projected into the action-head conditioning space
0175: and injected through AdaLN-style modulation, allowing temporal context to
0176: directly shape the flow-matching action distribution rather than serve only as
0177: passive historical context.
0178: We evaluate TFP on simulation benchmarks and real-world memory-dependent
0179: manipulation tasks. TFP consistently improves over reactive, history-based, and
0180: latent-state baselines. Mechanistic analyses further show that the learned
0181: belief stabilizes task-progress representations and causally modulates generated
0182: action chunks. Our contributions are:
0183: •
0184: We formulate stage-dependent manipulation as dynamics-aware belief
0185: tracking, where the correct action depends not only on latent task progress and
0186: interaction history, but also on when the internal belief should be revised or
0187: preserved.
0188: •
0189: We propose TFP, a belief-conditioned VLA framework that maintains a
0190: continuous-time latent belief using LTC dynamics and injects this belief
0191: directly into flow-matching action generation. We further introduce
0192: Episode-Aware Temporal Batching (EATB) to train recurrent belief states
0193: efficiently while preserving episode-local hidden-state continuity.
0194: •
0195: We show that TFP improves memory-dependent control in simulation and
0196: on a real Galaxea A1 robot, and provide mechanistic analyses showing that
0197: the learned belief stabilizes task-progress representations and causally
0198: modulates generated action chunks.

0263: Method
0264: TFP augments a chunked VLA policy with an episode-local latent belief over task
0265: progress and interaction history. At each policy query, the current visual and
0266: proprioceptive observations update this belief through Liquid Time-Constant
0267: (LTC) dynamics, and the updated belief directly conditions the flow-matching
0268: action head through AdaLN-style modulation. This couples memory and action
0269: generation: temporal context is not stored as a passive history buffer, but as a
0270: persistent state that modulates the next generated action chunk.

0807: Training with Episode-Aware Temporal Batching
0808: Training TFP requires preserving hidden-state continuity within each
0809: demonstration episode. Randomly shuffling individual chunks breaks this

2050: VI
2051: Conclusion
2052: We presented Temporally Conditioned Memory-Fusion Policies (TFP), a framework
2053: for extending reactive VLA policies with an explicit task-progress belief for
2054: history-dependent manipulation. TFP maintains this belief with Liquid
2055: Time-Constant dynamics and injects it directly into the flow-matching action
2056: decoder through AdaLN-style conditioning, allowing the learned belief to
2057: shape action generation rather than serve as passive history context. Across
2058: simulation and real-world tasks, TFP improves performance most clearly when the
2059: current observation alone is insufficient, including long-horizon, visually
2060: perturbed, occluded, and stage-dependent manipulation settings. Mechanistic
2061: analyses show that the learned write-gain dynamics change sharply near
2062: manipulation-event boundaries, while hidden-memory interventions show that
2063: changing only the belief can alter the generated action chunk and end-effector
2064: trajectory. These results suggest that memory in VLA policies should act as an
2065: event-sensitive action-conditioning state: retaining task progress during stable
2066: or ambiguous phases, and revising the belief when task-relevant evidence
2067: changes. Future work should combine such dynamics-aware memory with stronger
2068: object-centric grounding, spatial generalization, and compositional planning, while developing more efficient recurrent fine-tuning schemes.

Because each recurrent step may involve a large VLA backbone, an action-generation decoder, and a full action-horizon imitation loss, repeatedly calling the policy across many memory steps can substantially increase training cost and limit temporal parallelism.
2352: Self-attention provides parallel content-based access to history, but using past observations and action chunks as an ever-growing token set increases context length and does not by itself impose a physical-time belief dynamics
2353: [
2354: 31
2355: ]
2356: . Structured state-space models such as S4 and selective SSMs such as Mamba offer efficient long-sequence modeling with linearor near-linear scaling
2357: [
2358: 12
2359: ,
2360: 11
2361: ]
2362: . These methods are strong sequence encoders, but our setting requires more than long-context compression:the policy must maintain an online task-progress belief whose retention is calibrated by elapsed time and whose state directly conditions action generation.
2363: Continuous-time recurrent models provide another route to temporal memory.Classical continuous-time recurrent neural networks model hidden-state evolution with differential equations, while Neural ODEs, ODE-RNNs, and LatentODEs evolve hidden states continuously between irregular observations
2364: [
2365: 10
2366: ,
2367: 5
2368: ,
2369: 25
2370: ]
2371: .Liquid Time-Constant networks and closed-form continuous-time networks further parameterize input-dependent time constants for adaptive continuous-time sequence modeling
2372: [
2373: 13
2374: ,
2375: 14
2376: ]
2377: . TFP builds on this continuous-time memory family, but uses LTC not as a standalone sequence model:we use it as a compact belief filter for chunked VLA control, with vector-valued, input-dependent time constants and closed-form elapsed-time retention. The resulting memory is then projected into the action-head conditioning space, so temporal belief directly modulates the generated action chunk instead of remaining a passive history representation.
2378: Memory-augmented and history-aware visuomotor policies.
2379: Several recent works augment robot policies with history or memory to address partial observability and long-horizon manipulation. HAMLET adapts pretrained VLAs into history-aware policies by introducing moment tokens and a lightweight memory module that aggregates historical context for action prediction
2380: [
2381: 17
2382: ]
2383: . This provides an efficient alternative to naively appending multiple past frames, but its memory primarily serves as a token-level history compression and retrieval interface. MemoryVLA introduces a cognition-memory-action framework, where perceptual and cognitive tokens form working memory, retrieve decision-relevant entries from a memory bank, and condition a diffusion action expert
2384: [
2385: 27
2386: ]
2387: . This design preserves richer historical details, but the temporal state is organized around memory storage, retrieval, and consolidation rather than an explicit elapsed-time-conditioned task dynamics. AVA-VLA reformulates VLA control from a POMDP perspective and uses a recurrent belief state to actively modulate visual token processing
2388: [
2389: 33
2390: ]
2391: . However, its memory is mainly used to guide perception, whereas the action-generation pathway is not explicitly conditioned by a continuous-time task-progress belief.
2392: History-aware diffusion policies are also related. Causal Diffusion Policy conditions diffusion-based visuomotor control on historical action sequences through a causal transformer, improving temporal coherence and robustness under degraded observations
2393: [
2394: 20
2395: ]
2396: . Its focus is autoregressive action history modeling rather than language-conditioned VLA control, and its own limitation notes that extremely long-horizon tasks requiring sustained planning remain unresolved. These methods show that exposing the policy to better,richer, or more retrievable history is valuable. TFP addresses a complementary problem: the policy should not only access history, but should maintain a compact latent belief whose evolution follows temporal-conditioned task dynamics.
2397: Concretely, TFP compresses history into a fixed-size continuous-time belief state with input-dependent LTC retention and injects this belief directly into the generative action decoder through AdaLN-style modulation. Thus, memory is not only a retrieved context or an auxiliary visual-attention signal; it directly modulates the decoder activations and therefore the flow-matching velocity field over the predicted action chunk. This makes TFP better aligned with real manipulation dynamics, where the correct action depends on how task progress evolves over physical time, not merely on whether the model can attend to a more complete history.

2401: Memory-Conditioning Interface
2402: TFP conditions the action decoder through adaptive modulation rather than
2403: explicit memory-token cross-attention. Cross-attention-based memory injection
2404: requires the decoder to retrieve and interpret a separate set of memory tokens.

2407: embedding captures where the denoising process is, while the memory condition
2408: captures what temporal context is relevant. Their additive fusion lets the
2409: decoder jointly adapt to diffusion phase and retained task belief without adding
2410: an extra attention pathway.

2412: state rather than a retrievable history buffer. We do not claim that
2413: cross-attention is inherently inferior; instead, AdaLN provides a simpler

2595: Appendix C
2596: Continuous-Time Belief Interpretation
2597: This section formalizes the connection between Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) memory
2598: and belief filtering in partially observable manipulation. We do not claim that
2599: LTCs are universally more expressive than all recurrent models. A sufficiently
2600: large recurrent network with access to elapsed time may approximate many
2601: belief-update functions. Instead, our claim is more specific: LTC provides a
2602: structural inductive bias that matches a class of continuous-time,
2603: multi-timescale belief dynamics. This class is particularly relevant for
2604: chunked robot manipulation, where policy queries may occur after variable
2605: execution intervals.
2606: Claim.
2607: For manipulation tasks that can be modeled as partially observable decision
2608: processes with latent task state, the desired memory should behave as a
2609: time-consistent belief state. LTC implements the exact discretization of a
2610: first-order continuous-time belief relaxation process, and its exponential
2611: retention gate is the unique continuous scalar retention rule satisfying
2612: elapsed-time consistency. Step-indexed recurrent baselines such as GRUs or
2613: LSTMs do not enforce this property unless elapsed time is explicitly provided
2614: and the gate is constrained to satisfy the same exponential semigroup structure.

4115: D-B
4116: Failure Modes on MIKASA-Robo ShellGameTouch
4117: MIKASA-Robo ShellGameTouch provides a useful diagnostic for testing whether a
4118: policy can preserve information that is no longer visible in the current
4119: observation. In our same-backbone evaluation, TFP achieves the success rate of 75.0%, indicating that the learned belief
4120: state provides useful memory under occlusion. However, the remaining failures
4121: also reveal an important boundary of the proposed method: ShellGameTouch
4122: requires a memory regime that is only partially aligned with the event-sensitive
4123: manipulation dynamics targeted by TFP.
4124: A complementary memory regime.
4125: TFP is designed to model continuous task-progress dynamics in manipulation. Its
4126: LTC belief update is most useful when the policy must decide when to preserve
4127: slow task variables and when to revise them around interaction events such as
4128: contact, grasp, release, placement, or subgoal completion. ShellGameTouch has a
4129: different structure. The decisive variable is a discrete hidden object-location
4130: binding: which visually identical mug hides the ball. Once the ball is occluded,
4131: the current observation provides little corrective evidence about this latent
4132: identity-location association. The policy must therefore write the correct
4133: binding early and retrieve it at the final touch decision. This is closer to
4134: object-centric episodic memory or key-value binding than to continuously
4135: evolving manipulation-stage dynamics.
4136: Sparse opportunities for belief correction.
4137: In many manipulation tasks, the belief can be refined as new contact, motion,
4138: object-state, or subgoal-completion evidence appears. For example, after a
4139: grasp, release, or placement event, the observation gives new evidence about
4140: task progress, and an event-sensitive memory update can revise the internal
4141: state accordingly. In ShellGameTouch, by contrast, the crucial information is
4142: available only before and during occlusion. After the mugs become visually
4143: ambiguous, later observations mainly support execution rather than correction
4144: of the hidden ball-location belief. As a result, a weak or incorrect initial
4145: memory write is difficult to repair through subsequent LTC updates.
4146: Representation mismatch.
4147: This failure mode can also be viewed through a representation-learning lens.
4148: Prior work on interpreting neural robot policies argues that useful policy
4149: representations often align with task-relevant factors of variation, such as
4150: skills, behaviors, or strategies
4151: [
4152: 32
4153: ]
4154: . Under this view,
4155: ShellGameTouch stresses a different dominant factor of variation from the one
4156: most directly targeted by TFP. The task-relevant factor in ShellGameTouch is a
4157: persistent categorical object-location variable, whereas the factor targeted by
4158: TFP is event-sensitive task-progress dynamics. TFP can still improve
4159: ShellGameTouch because its belief state preserves useful latent context, but
4160: the memory is not explicitly structured as a symbolic object map, slot memory,
4161: or key-value store over hidden cup identities.
4162: Failure categories.
4163: Qualitatively, we observe four main failure modes. First, the policy may fail
4164: to encode the correct ball–mug association before occlusion, especially when
4165: the relevant visual cue is brief or spatially small. Second, the memory may
4166: retain a coarse task-progress signal but not a sufficiently precise
4167: object-location binding, causing the final touch to target the wrong mug.
4168: Third, the retained belief may be correct, but the action decoder may fail to
4169: ground that belief into the correct end-effector target under visually similar
4170: mugs. Finally, some failures are low-level execution errors near the final
4171: target rather than clear memory errors. These categories suggest that
4172: ShellGameTouch requires both persistent memory and precise object-centric
4173: grounding.
4174: Interpretation.
4175: We therefore treat ShellGameTouch as a same-backbone diagnostic rather than a
4176: direct state-of-the-art comparison. The improvement over the reactive
4177: baselines shows that TFP provides useful memory under occlusion, but
4178: the absolute success rate reflects the mismatch between ShellGameTouch’s
4179: categorical hidden-location memory and TFP’s primary design target:
4180: continuous-time, event-sensitive manipulation belief dynamics. A natural future
4181: direction is to combine TFP with an object-centric memory module, such as a
4182: slot-based belief map or a retrieval mechanism for discrete hidden-state
4183: bindings. Such a hybrid would allow the policy to retain symbolic
4184: object-location facts while still using LTC dynamics to update task-progress
4185: belief around manipulation events.
