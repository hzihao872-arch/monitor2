0029: Abstract
0030: Generalist robot policies offer useful initialization, but refining compact
0031: specialists through limited physical interaction requires informative
0032: learning feedback. We present FIERCE, a generalist-initialized reinforcement
0033: learning framework centered on a unified, task-adaptive progress–failure
0034: evaluator. Its architecture shares an observation–language representation
0035: between an observed-progress head and an action-conditioned latent
0036: predictor whose past and current predictions feed a causal sequence head
0037: for task-failure estimation.
0038: Joint supervision from progress and preference labels, synchronized
0039: commands and observations, and terminal outcomes trains the evaluator;
0040: target-task rollouts support adaptation and calibration. Fixed evaluator
0041: snapshots provide progress shaping and failure-risk penalties alongside
0042: independently verified terminal rewards, while evaluator and policy updates
0043: alternate as new experience is collected. Refinement requires neither
0044: continued generalist action queries nor a dedicated target-task simulator
0045: or manually annotated dense rewards. Only the compact specialist is
0046: retained at deployment. The evaluation separates feedback quality,
0047: policy-learning efficiency, and deployment cost across simulation and two
0048: contact-rich real tasks. Code, model weights, and data-restoration tools are released at
0049: https://github.com/ar-mine/FIERCE
0050: .

0121: rather than reproducing the generalist more faithfully. Independently
0122: verified terminal outcomes establish whether an attempt succeeds, but do

0127: The difficulty is that local progress and eventual success need not
0128: agree. For example, pushing a misaligned connector toward a socket may
0129: appear to advance insertion while increasing the chance of jamming.
0130: Retracting to realign may temporarily reduce progress yet improve the
0131: chance of completing the task. Progress feedback describes how much an
0132: observed execution advances the task; prospective failure feedback
0133: estimates the risk associated with a proposed action and subsequent
0134: execution. A useful learning signal must distinguish these roles while
0135: remaining grounded in real transitions and independently verified
0136: outcomes.
0137: Robometer demonstrates that progress and trajectory-preference
0138: feedback can transfer across robot experience
0139: [

0144: support terminally supervised failure detection
0145: [
0146: 9
0147: ]

0149: Motivated by these findings, we study a unified, task-adaptive
0150: progress–failure evaluator as a feedback interface for specialist
0151: learning. The central empirical question is whether learned failure
0152: scores add value to progress shaping and terminal rewards during
0153: physical refinement. Comparisons with progress-based and terminal-reward
0154: learning assess the overall pipeline; a within-architecture ablation
0155: isolates the use of the failure penalty while retaining failure-head
0156: supervision.
0157: We introduce
0158: FIERCE
0159: (
0160: Failure-Informed Efficient Reinforcement
0161: Learning for Compact Experts
0162: ), a generalist-initialized RL framework
0163: for refining compact robot specialists through real-world interaction
0164: (Fig.
0165: 1
0166: ). Initialization supplies a compact
0167: policy; subsequent improvement uses its own rollouts without requiring
0168: further generalist action queries. The framework centers on a task-adaptive,
0169: action-conditioned progress–failure evaluator. Its shared encoder
0170: feeds an observed-progress head and a latent-prediction pathway with a
0171: causal risk readout. Multi-task post-training uses diverse trajectories
0172: and audited action-synchronized data; target-task fitting and score
0173: calibration refresh this feedback using local successes and failures.
0174: Progress increments, a failure-risk penalty, and verified
0175: terminal rewards relabel real transitions for RL policy updates.
0176: Evaluator and policy updates alternate as new experience becomes available.
0177: Target-task learning requires no dedicated simulator, manually annotated
0178: dense rewards, or separate sim-to-real policy-transfer stage. At
0179: deployment, both the generalist and the large evaluator are removed,
0180: leaving a standalone specialist.
0181: Our contributions are twofold:
0182: •
0183: A unified progress–failure evaluator design with separate
0184: observed-progress and action-conditioned predictive pathways, trained
0185: as a refreshable feedback interface for specialist RL.
0186: •
0187: A physical-refinement framework for compact policies initialized
0188: from demonstrations, including generalist rollouts, with no continued
0189: generalist action queries or dedicated target-task simulator.
0190: Deployment retains only the policy; within-architecture comparisons
0191: assess failure feedback and evaluator-training stages.
0192: We assess progress and failure discrimination, feedback-guided policy
0193: learning, and the resulting specialists’ task-performance and inference-cost
0194: trade-offs. Alternative-action validity is distinct from these evaluations.

1696: action-conditioned latent prediction provide different learning signals;
1697: local adaptation and refreshes fit them to new execution data. The
1698: reported within-architecture ablation favors using the failure penalty
1699: on both real tasks, while deployment retains only the compact actor.
1700: These are single-seed observations, not evidence of robustness to retraining
1701: or reduced total robot operating cost.
1702: The study does not isolate parameter sharing from independently trained
1703: progress and failure models, and its discrimination and RL metrics do not
1704: establish policy-wise probability calibration or same-state alternative-action
1705: ranking. Risk penalties can discourage useful corrective actions; policy shift
1706: and hidden contact states remain limitations. We claim no formal safety,
1707: optimal-policy invariance, or early-recovery guarantee. The latency
1708: measurement concerns the deployed actor, not evaluator compute or
1709: successful task throughput. Data, weights, restoration tools, split manifests, and evaluation
1710: scripts will be released subject to source licenses.
