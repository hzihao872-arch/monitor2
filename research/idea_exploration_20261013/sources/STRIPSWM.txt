0029: Abstract
0030: Robots performing long-horizon visual manipulation observe high-dimensional images, but
0031: successful plans depend on action-relevant facts: what can be done now and what changes
0032: afterward. Classical task planners exploit this structure through symbolic operators with
0033: preconditions and effects, but obtaining such representations from raw visual experience
0034: remains challenging. We study a visual task-planning setting in which a robot receives only
0035: action-image transitions: the current image, executed high-level action, and resulting image.
0036: At test time, given start and goal images, the robot must produce a sequence of high-level
0037: actions that reaches the goal. We introduce
0038: strips-wm
0039: , a framework for learning image-grounded
0040: strips
0041: -style world models directly from visual transitions.
0042: strips-wm
0043: first induces a finite
0044: abstract transition graph from images, then learns binary predicates and propositional
0045: operators that explain this graph. The learned predicates are grounded back into perception through a visual classifier, enabling classical
0046: planning directly from novel start and goal images. Experiments on visual rearrangement
0047: tasks show that
0048: strips-wm
0049: improves image-to-plan success over the tested visual rollout, latent graph-search, and latent-symbolic baselines.
