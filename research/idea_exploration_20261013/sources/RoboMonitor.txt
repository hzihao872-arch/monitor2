0049: Abstract
0050: Learned robot policies produce actions, but their outputs alone do not establish whether execution is progressing as intended. Robot execution monitoring requires identifying the current execution
0051: phase, detecting failures, and recognizing task completion from
0052: observations available during execution. Training such monitors requires
0053: annotations that are scarce in datasets collected for robot-policy
0054: learning. We present RoboMonitor, a label-efficient vision–language
0055: execution monitor that learns from these datasets before introducing
0056: monitoring supervision. We pre-train on 25 hours of multi-camera
0057: trajectories spanning 12 manipulation tasks and two robot embodiments,
0058: using action-conditioned future-feature prediction, inverse dynamics,
0059: and masked-present prediction. We then transfer the learned visual and
0060: context encoders to a causal monitor and apply temporal supervised
0061: fine-tuning (Temporal SFT), which combines supervision throughout each
0062: observation window with consistency objectives within and across
0063: overlapping windows. At deployment, RoboMonitor requires only the task
0064: instruction and camera observations.
0065: On a four-task monitoring benchmark, RoboMonitor trained with 52 labeled
0066: episodes achieves 93.1% mean phase accuracy and 85.9% macro recall over
0067: two fine-tuning seeds, exceeding Qwen3-VL and Robometer trained with the
0068: same monitoring supervision. Its phase accuracy also exceeds that of both Qwen3-VL and
0069: Robometer trained with 100 episodes. A Qwen3-VL ablation shows that
0070: Temporal SFT reduces mean spurious phase switching from 15.23% to
0071: 4.95%. In closed-loop deployment, the integrated system completes
0072: 39 of 40 simulated Toolbox Sorting trials and 35 of 40 real-world Reel
0073: Packing trials, with no false recovery triggers observed.
0074: Fig. 1:
0075: Overview of RoboMonitor.
0076: Training has two stages: (1) predictive
0077: self-supervised pre-training on unlabeled robot trajectories, which uses
0078: multi-view observations together with recorded robot state and executed
0079: actions to learn execution-relevant visual and temporal representations; and
0080: (2) causal supervised fine-tuning (Temporal SFT) on a smaller
0081: monitoring-labeled dataset to predict execution phase, failure, and task
0082: completion. At deployment, RoboMonitor receives only the task instruction and
0083: camera observations, so it can monitor both end-to-end visuomotor policies and
0084: task-orchestration systems without access to the underlying controller.

0088: manipulation

0108: In either setting, execution requires feedback about the physical
0109: outcome of the robot’s actions. A grasp may miss, an object may be
0110: dropped, or a skill may terminate before achieving its intended effect.
0111: An execution monitor provides this feedback by identifying the current
0112: phase, detecting failures, and recognizing task completion from the task
0113: instruction and recent observations. These predictions can inform task
0114: progression, recovery, replanning, or human intervention. Their temporal
0115: stability also matters: phase estimates that fluctuate while the robot
0116: remains in the same execution stage can give an orchestrator inconsistent
0117: information about task progress.
0118: Training such a monitor requires dense temporal annotations that are rarely available in robot-learning datasets.
0119: Policy datasets contain large amounts of synchronized video, robot state, and action data, but are dominated by
0120: nominal executions and generally do not indicate the current phase, the onset of a failure, or when the overall task has completed. Collecting representative failures and annotating trajectories frame by frame is costly.
0121: However, the large amount of robot data already collected during policy development provides an opportunity to learn relevant representations of the task before monitoring annotations are introduced.
0122: We introduce
0123: RoboMonitor
0124: (
0125: Figure 1
0126: ), a two-stage approach to
0127: label-efficient execution monitoring. First, predictive pre-training
0128: adapts the visual encoder of a vision–language model (VLM) and
0129: learns a temporal context encoder from multi-camera robot trajectories. The training
0130: objective combines future-feature prediction conditioned on recorded
0131: robot state and commands with inverse dynamics and masked-present
0132: prediction. We then transfer the visual and context encoders to a causal
0133: monitor and fine-tune it on trajectories annotated with execution phase,
0134: failure, and task completion. Our Temporal SFT procedure supervises the monitor’s prediction at every timestep of each observation window rather than only at the final one, and encourages consistent predictions within and across overlapping windows. Robot state and commands are confined to pre-training; the deployed monitor uses the task instruction and camera observations.
0135: Our contributions are: (i) a predictive pre-training and representation
0136: transfer approach that learns from robot trajectories without monitoring
0137: annotations; (ii) a causal execution monitor trained with dense
0138: supervision and temporal consistency objectives; and (iii) an evaluation
0139: of monitoring accuracy and stability at two annotation budgets, together
0140: with closed-loop execution and recovery in simulation and on a real
0141: robot.

1372: V
1373: Experiments
1374: We evaluate three questions:
1375: Q1 (Label efficiency):
1376: Does RoboMonitor improve execution monitoring when labeled data
1377: are limited?
1378: Q2 (Temporal stability):
1379: Does Temporal SFT reduce spurious phase switching compared with
1380: final-readout-only supervision?
1381: Q3 (Closed-loop execution):
1382: Can RoboMonitor support closed-loop task execution and failure recovery within a task-orchestration system?
1383: V-A
1384: Data and Tasks
1385: Self-supervised corpus.
1386: We pre-train RoboMonitor on 25 hours of multi-camera robot
1387: trajectories collected during policy development, spanning
1388: 12 manipulation tasks and two robot embodiments. Pre-training
1389: uses synchronized RGB observations, recorded robot state, and
1390: commanded actions, without phase, failure, or task-completion
1391: annotations.
1392: Supervised monitoring tasks.
1393: We train a shared execution monitor on four manipulation tasks:
1394: Reel Packing.
1395: One arm inserts its gripper into a reel to pick it up and transfers
1396: the reel to the other arm, which places it in a black tote.
1397: The annotated phases are

1403: Place

1407: and inserts it into its testing module. The annotated phases
1408: are
1409: Pick
1410: ,
1411: Transport
1412: , and
1413: Insert
1414: .
1415: Toolbox Sorting.
1416: The robot sorts tools and small parts, including screwdrivers,
1417: nuts, and bolts, into designated areas in Isaac Sim. The annotated
1418: phases are
1419: None
1420: ,
1421: Pick
1422: ,
1423: Transport
1424: ,
1425: and
1426: Place
1427: .
1428: Humanoid Grasping.
1429: The humanoid repositions a bin by pushing it to accommodate the
1430: arms’ limited reach when transferring an object between them.
1431: The annotated phases are
1432: Push
1433: ,

1437: Place

1439: Reel Packing and Electronic Component Insertion are represented

1441: Humanoid Grasping are absent. All four tasks are included during
1442: supervised fine-tuning. The monitor uses the union of their phase
1443: labels as a shared seven-class vocabulary, with separate binary
1444: outputs for failure and task completion.
1445: V-B
1446: Offline Evaluation
1447: We compare supervised training budgets of 52 and 100 labeled
1448: episodes across the four tasks. The 100-episode set contains 27 Reel Packing, 26 Humanoid
1449: Grasping, 25 Toolbox Sorting, and 22 Electronic Component
1450: Insertion episodes. The 52-episode set contains
1451: 13 episodes per task and is nested within the larger set.
1452: All methods are evaluated on the same held-out benchmark,
1453: comprising 66 episodes and 7,022 observation windows. We
1454: construct this benchmark to stress-test execution monitoring
1455: across varied execution conditions. It combines nominal
1456: executions, unsuccessful policy rollouts, and teleoperated
1457: trajectories containing execution errors, including runs at
1458: different controller update frequencies. This mixture tests
1459: the models’ ability to recognize execution phases and distinguish
1460: failures from successful behavior across variation in execution
1461: timing and rollout quality. Each evaluation query contains
1462: the task instruction and an eight-timestep causal observation
1463: window from the available two or three camera streams.
1464: For each fine-tuned configuration at each evaluated budget,
1465: we report the mean and standard deviation over two SFT seeds.
1466: Each run is evaluated using its validation-selected checkpoint,
1467: and all methods use the same scoring procedure.

1520: We deploy RoboMonitor in a graph-based task orchestrator similar to GaP
1521: [

1526: V-E
1527: Implementation Details
1528: Predictive pre-training is performed on eight NVIDIA H100 GPUs,
1529: and supervised fine-tuning uses four GPUs. The real-world
1530: execution monitor runs on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q GPU. During deployment, the monitor receives only the task instruction and camera observations; robot state and commands are not required.
1531: V-F
1532: Metrics
1533: We report phase accuracy and macro recall, task-completion
1534: average precision (AP), failure AP, and spurious-switch rate.
1535: A spurious switch occurs when the predicted phase changes
1536: between adjacent observations while the ground-truth phase
1537: remains unchanged; lower is better. For closed-loop evaluation,
1538: we report failure-detection recall, false recovery triggers,
1539: recovery success, task-completion rate, and inference latency.
1540: VI
1541: Results
1542: VI-A
1543: Label-Efficient Monitoring (Q1)
1544: Table I
1545: reports monitoring performance
1546: at the two annotation budgets. With 52 labeled episodes,
1547: RoboMonitor achieves 93.09% mean phase accuracy and 85.85%
1548: macro recall. These exceed Qwen3-VL with Temporal SFT by
1549: 6.33 and 11.62 percentage points, respectively, and Robometer
1550: with Temporal SFT by 4.32 and 4.07 points. RoboMonitor also
1551: achieves the highest task-completion AP at this budget,
1552: 87.60%, compared with 85.42% for Robometer and 74.01%
1553: for Qwen3-VL.
1554: The cross-budget comparison further supports label efficiency
1555: for phase recognition. RoboMonitor trained with 52 episodes
1556: exceeds the mean phase accuracy of Robometer trained with
1557: 100 episodes (93.09% versus 91.65%), while its macro recall
1558: is within 0.37 percentage points (85.85% versus 86.22%). RoboMonitor’s 52-episode model also exceeds Qwen3-VL trained
1559: with 100 episodes in phase accuracy (93.09% versus 91.19%)
1560: and macro recall (85.85% versus 82.73%).
1561: RoboMonitor therefore achieves competitive phase recognition
1562: with fewer labeled episodes on this benchmark.
1563: Failure detection shows a different pattern at the smaller
1564: budget. With 52 episodes, RoboMonitor obtains 45.87%
1565: failure AP, below Robometer’s 59.39% and close to
1566: Qwen3-VL’s 46.37%. With 100 episodes, RoboMonitor reaches
1567: 93.63% phase accuracy, 88.55% macro recall, 92.29%
1568: task-completion AP, and 72.01% failure AP. All four means exceed the corresponding Robometer and Qwen3-VL
1569: results. Failure AP is higher by 4.33 and 11.05 percentage points,
1570: respectively. The low-label advantage
1571: is clearest for phase recognition and completion; stronger
1572: failure detection is observed at the larger budget.
1573: Gemini Robotics ER 2 achieves 74.67% phase accuracy and
1574: 61.19% macro recall, below all fine-tuned configurations.
1575: Its few-shot result provides a reference for monitoring with
1576: labeled in-context examples and no parameter updates.
1577: VI-B
1578: Temporal Stability (Q2)
1579: At the 52-episode budget, Qwen3-VL with Temporal SFT reduces
1580: mean spurious-switch rate from 15.23% to 4.95% relative
1581: to Pointwise SFT, a reduction of 10.28 percentage points.
1582: Failure AP increases from 30.34% to 46.37%, while phase
1583: accuracy increases more modestly, from 85.42% to 86.76%.
1584: Thus, the reduction in switching is accompanied by improved
1585: phase accuracy and failure AP. This ablation supports the
1586: combined dense-supervision and temporal-consistency recipe.
1587: Across the Temporal SFT configurations, phase accuracy and
1588: switching rate favor different methods at the smaller budget.
1589: RoboMonitor has the highest phase accuracy with 52 episodes,
1590: while its spurious-switch rate is 5.58%, compared with
1591: 5.27% for Robometer and 4.95% for Qwen3-VL.
1592: With 100 episodes, RoboMonitor achieves the lowest mean switching rate, 3.17%, compared with 3.76% for Robometer
1593: and 3.58% for Qwen3-VL. Switching rate should
1594: therefore be interpreted alongside phase accuracy when
1595: assessing execution-monitoring performance.

1638: in either setting.
1639: Inference takes approximately 180–200 ms per observation
1640: window. These trials demonstrate the use of RoboMonitor for
1641: task progression and predefined recovery in simulation and
1642: on a real robot. These deployments complement the four-task offline stress test
1643: by measuring failure-event detection, recovery, and task completion
1644: within the integrated monitor–orchestrator system on two tasks.
1645: VII
1646: Conclusion
1647: We presented RoboMonitor, a label-efficient execution monitor that combines
1648: predictive self-supervised pre-training with Temporal SFT. Across four
1649: downstream manipulation tasks, RoboMonitor achieves 93.09% mean phase
1650: accuracy and 85.85% macro recall with 52 labeled episodes, exceeding the
1651: phase accuracy of the evaluated Qwen3-VL and Robometer baselines trained
1652: with 100 episodes. An ablation of the supervised training objective further
1653: shows that Temporal SFT reduces the Qwen3-VL spurious-switch rate from
1654: 15.23% to 4.95% and increases failure AP from 30.34% to 46.37%
1655: relative to Pointwise SFT. In closed-loop deployment, the integrated system
1656: completes 39 of 40 simulated Toolbox Sorting trials and 35 of 40 real-world
1657: Reel Packing trials, with no false recovery triggers observed in either
1658: setting.
1659: A limitation of the current formulation is that it assigns one global
1660: execution phase to each timestep. This represents coordinated bimanual
1661: behaviors such as handover, but cannot express asynchronous execution in
1662: which two manipulators independently perform different subtasks at the same
1663: time. Future work could instead use factorized or multi-label execution
1664: states for individual manipulators, objects, or concurrent skills. We also
1665: plan to augment the monitor’s categorical outputs with visually grounded
1666: language reasoning

1671: ]
1672: , allowing the planner to receive a
1673: description of what changed in the scene in addition to a failure score.
1674: Finally, conformal methods

1678: could provide calibrated
