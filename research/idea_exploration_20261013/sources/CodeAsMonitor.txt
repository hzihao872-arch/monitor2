0068: Abstract
0069: Automatic detection and prevention of open-set failures are crucial in closed-loop robotic systems.
0070: Recent studies often struggle to simultaneously identify unexpected failures reactively after they occur and prevent foreseeable ones proactively.
0071: To this end, we propose Code-as-Monitor (CaM), a novel paradigm leveraging the vision-language model (VLM) for both open-set reactive and proactive failure detection.
0072: The core of our method is to formulate both tasks as a unified set of spatio-temporal constraint satisfaction problems and use VLM-generated code to evaluate them for real-time monitoring.
0073: To enhance the accuracy and efficiency of monitoring, we further introduce constraint elements that abstract constraint-related entities or their parts into compact geometric elements.
0074: This approach offers greater generality, simplifies tracking, and facilitates constraint-aware visual programming by leveraging these elements as visual prompts.
0075: Experiments show that CaM achieves a
0076: 28.7
0077: %
0078: 28.7\%
0079: higher success rate and reduces execution time by
0080: 31.8
0081: %
0082: 31.8\%
0083: under severe disturbances compared to baselines across three simulators and a real-world setting.
0084: Moreover, CaM can be integrated with open-loop control policies to form closed-loop systems, enabling long-horizon tasks in cluttered scenes with dynamic environments.
0085: See the project page at
0086: https://zhoues.github.io/Code-as-Monitor

0144: Introduction
0145: As expectations grow for robots to handle long-horizon tasks within intricate environments, failures are unavoidable.
0146: Therefore, automatically detecting and preventing those failures plays a vital role in ensuring the tasks can eventually be solved, especially for closed-loop robotic systems.
0147: There are two modes of failure detection

0154: proactive

0211: However, these methods often bear compromised execution speeds and coarse-grained detection accuracy, due to the high computational costs and inadequate 3D spatio-temporal perception capability of recent LLMs/VLMs.
0212: Moreover, open-set
0213: proactive
0214: failure detection, which has been less explored in the literature, presents even severer challenges as it is required to foresee potential causes of failure and monitor them in real-time with high precision to anticipate and prevent imminent failure.
0215: Simply adapting LLMs/VLMs cannot meet such expectations.
0216: In this work, we aim to develop an open-set failure detection framework that achieves reactive and proactive detection simultaneously, not only benefiting from the generalization power of VLMs but also enjoying high precision in monitoring failure characteristics with real-time efficiency.
0217: We address this by formulating both tasks as a unified set of spatio-temporal constraint satisfaction problems, which can be
0218: precisely

0220: Such visual programs can efficiently verify whether entities (

0227: To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt to integrate both detection modes within a single framework.
0228: We name this constraint-aware visual programming framework as Code-as-Monitor (
0229: CaM
0230: ).
0231: To be specific, the proposed spatio-temporal constraint satisfaction scheme abstracts the constraint-related entity or part segments from the observed images into compact geometric elements (
0232: e.g.
0233: , points, lines, and surfaces), as shown in
0234: Fig.
0235: 1

0237: It simplifies the monitoring of constraint satisfaction by tracking and evaluating the spatio-temporal combinational dynamics of these elements, eliminating the most irrelevant geometric and visual details of the raw entities/parts.
0238: The constraint element detection and tracking are grounded by our trained constraint-aware segmentation and off-the-shelf tracking models, ensuring speed, accuracy, and certain open-set adaptation capabilities.
0239: The evaluation protocol is in the form of VLM-generated code,
0240: i.e.
0241: , monitor code, which is visually prompted by the starting frames of a sub-goal and its associated constraint elements, in addition to the textual constraints that must be fulfilled.
0242: Once generated, this monitor code could detect reactive or proactive failures just by being executed according to the tracked constraint elements, without needing to call the VLMs again.
0243: Therefore, this minimalist scheme is generalizable to open-set failure detection for unseen entities and scenes (enabled by the potential diversity of the structured associations of constraint elements) as well as common skills (powered by the rich prior knowledge offered by VLMs), maintaining sufficient detection accuracy and real-time execution speed.

2118: Conclusion
2119: In this paper, we present a novel paradigm termed Code-as-Monitor, leveraging the VLMs for both open-set reactive and proactive failure detection.
2120: In detail, We formulate both detection modes as spatio-temporal constraint satisfaction problems and use VLM-generated code to evaluate them for real-time monitoring.
2121: We further propose constraint elements, which abstract constraints-related entities or their parts into compact geometric elements, to improve the precision and efficiency of monitoring.
2122: Extensive experiments demonstrate the superiority of the proposed approach and highlight its potential to advance closed-loop robot systems.

2555: More discussions
2556: A.1
2557: Methodology discussions
2558: What contributes to generalization.
2559: VLM’s intrinsic knowledge and reasoning enable task-level generalization, while geometric abstraction ensures generalization across scenes and entities (
2560: e.g.
2561: , objects, robots).
2562: The constraint-based formulation seamlessly integrates both aspects, achieving overall framework generalization.
2563: Proactive detection for long-term dynamics.
2564: Our framework can potentially handle long-term dynamics by leveraging the Constraint Generator to decompose long-horizon tasks into subgoals and enables proactive detection within each subgoal to
2565: constrain the future state space
2566: rather than directly predict future states.
2567: Moreover, failed subgoals are re-planned with updated proactive constraints, ensuring adaptability to long-term dynamic changes.
2568: Key methodology compared to Rekep
2569: [
2570: 21
2571: ]
2572: .
2573: Our key method differs from ReKep in two key aspects:
2574: (1) Element extraction:
2575: ReKep simply relies on DINOv2 for keypoint detection via 2D feature clustering, which offers a limited representation of constraints and geometric relationships crucial for failure detection.
2576: In contrast, our method achieves higher-order geometric abstraction by leveraging
2577: ConSeg
2578: to extract constraint-aware objects/parts in 2D space, then combining these with point clouds to integrate 3D geometric constraints.
2579: (2) Code Generation:
2580: Unlike ReKep, which lacks fine-grained guidance, our method leverages these
2581: geometry-rich elements
2582: as visual prompts on
2583: multi-view
2584: images to generate code more accurately
2585: at each subgoal’s beginning
2586: , enabling seamless integration with various policies to form closed-loop systems.
2587: Part segmentation for ambiguous or incomplete instructions.
2588: In our framework, we mainly leverage
2589: ConSeg
2590: ’s abilities to handle cases where instructions with ambiguous or missing explicit part information.
2591: (1)
2592: We do not impose whether the textual instructions contain part information in our dataset collection pipeline.
2593: This allows the
2594: ConSeg
2595: to possess a certain ability to handle ambiguous instructions.
2596: (2)
2597: ConSeg
2598: architecture adopts a VLM-based design, leveraging VLM’s world knowledge to infer constraint-related parts for unseen objects.
2599: Based on our experiments (see Supp.
2600: F.4
2601: ),
2602: ConSeg
2603: does demonstrate some generalization ability, but its performance on unseen objects is weaker than on objects within the training distribution.
2604: A.2
2605: Technical detail discussions
2606: Executability and reliability of code.
2607: For executability, we validate the code using the path coverage of White-box Testing at the subgoal’s beginning, where the current state is used as input to verify the accuracy of each logic branch (
2608: i.e.
2609: , every if-else block). If any error occurs during testing, the code is regenerated.
2610: For reliability, we prompt VLM to generate code that closely adheres to the given specifications and requirements.
2611: As frequent VLM calls increase hallucinations, we only invoke VLM once at each subgoal’s beginning, making it less affected by hallucination and more reliable.
2612: Failure threshold Selection.
2613: We use two ways:
2614: (1)
2615: manually initializing thresholds for common tasks in an external knowledge base;
2616: (2)
2617: generating thresholds for unseen tasks using VLM’s internal knowledge.
2618: We find that the system remains robust to threshold selection in common settings, but the impact of threshold variations under diverse conditions remains unexplored and is left for future work.
2619: Computational cost.
2620: The segmentation model is only invoked at the subgoal’s beginning to generate elements and
2621: excluded during execution
2622: , ensuring real-time code-based monitoring by tracking only the elements without high computational cost.
2623: We also perform parallel inference of segmentation models to accelerate at the subgoal’s beginning.
2624: A.3
2625: Limitations and Future Work
2626: Despite promising results, our approach has several limitations.
2627: First, using Visual Language Models (VLMs)—such as off-the-shelf GPT for code generation and constraint-aware segmentation models—inevitably leads to hallucination issues. Even with minimized VLM usage, inaccuracies in code generation and biases in segmentation may persist. Integrating more relevant knowledge via methods like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
2628: [
2629: 30
2630: ]
2631: or reducing multimodal large language model (MLLM) hallucinations
2632: [
2633: 42
2634: ]
2635: could alleviate these problems.
2636: Second, while we unify reactive and proactive failure detection as spatio-temporal constraint satisfaction problems and propose constraint elements for simplified real-time, high-precision monitoring, the constraint element representation has limitations:
2637: (1) It primarily focuses on failures detectable through explicit displacement and rotation, rendering it less effective for force-direction-related failures without noticeable displacement—for example, a robotic gripper failing to open a drawer due to incorrect force application.
2638: (2) The simplified representation abstracts objects and minimizes irrelevant visual details for efficient failure detection but may overlook critical visual cues and multimodal inputs, such as flowing water or audible sounds from a partially closed faucet, which are ignored in our current framework.
2639: Thus, exploring more robust representations that balance real-time precision with minimal information loss or integrate richer multimodal inputs is a promising direction for future research.

2882: , it will not immediately pick up a dropped block during movement but will continue with the previously planned actions).
2883: In Omnigibson, We utilize ReKep
2884: [
2885: 21
2886: ]
2887: as our low-level control policy, transforming long-horizon tasks into a set of relationships between fixed keypoints at different stages.
2888: At each stage, an optimization algorithm computes these relationships to generate actions, enabling language-conditioned closed-loop control.
2889: Notably, ReKep employs a pre-trained large vision model (
2890: i.e.
2891: , DINOv2
2892: [
2893: 45
2894: ]
2895: ) to process raw RGB data, extracting semantically relevant keypoints.
2896: This approach also serves as the compared method in our ablation study for extracting 3D points through constraint-aware segmentation, showing our superiority.
2897: In RLBench, we employ the Autoregressive Policy (ARP)
2898: [
2899: 73
2900: ]
2901: as the control policy, which generates the next action based on historical observations and action sequences through an autoregressive process.
2902: This method achieves state-of-the-art performance in the RLBench.
2903: In the real-world setting, We employ DexGraspNet 2.0
2904: [
2905: 72
2906: ]
2907: as our low-level policy, which predicts the dexterous hand’s grasping pose based on the scene’s point cloud and facilitates the robotic arm’s action trajectory through motion planning to achieve robust generalized grasping.
2908: Notably, DexGraspNet 2.0 is an open-loop policy, which means it does not adjust to environmental changes during action execution.
2909: For example, if the target object’s position shifts while the arm moves, the system does not modify its motion plan.
2910: Therefore, it continues to execute toward the originally intended location to complete the grasp, failing.

3948: Task, Disturbance and Metric Details
3949: We conduct real-world evaluations on two tasks:
3950: (1)
3951: Simple Pick & Place: The robot should pick and place objects within
3952: 70
3953: 70
3954: seconds.
3955: We include four kinds of objects: Deformable, Transparent, Small Rigid, and Large Geometric, with three objects in each category.
3956: The deformable objects are Loopy, Dog, and Rabbit toys, which undergo deformation when grasped by the dexterous hand.
3957: The transparent objects are a clear beverage bottle, a transparent glass, and a clear shampoo bottle.
3958: The small rigid objects consist of apple, pear, and peach models, while the large geometric objects include a large plate, ball, and pyramid.
3959: (2)
3960: Reasoning Pick & Place: In cluttered scenes, the robot must perform long-horizon tasks where the instructions contain ambiguous terms (
3961: e.g.
3962: , “animals” or “fruits” without specifying particular types).
3963: Specifically, the tasks are: (a) “Clear all objects on the table except for
3964: animals
3965: ”, and (b) ”Grasp the
3966: animals
3967: according to their distances to
3968: fruits
3969: , from nearest to farthest”, with ambiguous terms underlined.
3970: For both tasks, we introduce two identical disturbances:
3971: (1)
3972: Moving the object during the robot’s grasping.
3973: (2)
3974: Removing the object from the robot’s dexterous hand during movement after grasping.
3975: For each task, and each object involved in Simple Pick & Place, we conduct 10 trials.
3976: For each long-horizon task in Reasoning Pick & Place, we also conduct 10 trials.
3977: We evaluate performance based on success rate and execution time,
3978: including
3979: the computational time invoking the VLM.
3980: Results are reported as mean values with
3981: 95
3982: %
3983: 95\%
3984: confidence intervals.
3985: For the Simple Pick & Place, the robot has only one opportunity to autonomously release the object held in its gripper at a designated location.
3986: Any disturbance the robot encounters allows for a return and reattempt at grasping if the robot successfully detects it.
3987: Success is defined as meeting these conditions within
3988: 90
3989: 90
3990: seconds.
3991: For the Reasoning Pick & Place task, the robot must clear all objects on the table except for animals within
3992: 4
3993: 4
3994: minutes for “Clear all objects on the table except for animals”.
3995: In the task “Grasp the animals according to their distances to fruits, from nearest to farthest”, the robot must sequentially grasp the animals in the correct order within
3996: 2
3997: 2
3998: minutes, despite human-induced distractions such as moving animals or fruits.
3999: Notably, this task is particularly challenging because the robot operates under an open-loop policy, preventing it from using closed-loop feedback to handle the dynamic distances between fruits and animals caused by external disturbances.
4000: Therefore, a failure detection framework is necessary to enable both
4001: reactive
4002: and
4003: proactive
4004: real-time detection with high precision, monitoring the distance changes and adjusting the grasping sequence accordingly.
