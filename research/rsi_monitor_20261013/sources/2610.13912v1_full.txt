Abstract

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) aims to transform interaction experience into persistent improvements, yet external feedback is costly and world-model predictions may become unreliable as policies evolve. We introduce RSI-Anything, a domain-general framework built on JEPA-Anything that uses predictive world models to guide action and learning-data selection. Observed feedback drives policy or workflow improvement and conditional world-model adaptation, while component and compatibility checks govern the retention of updated policy–model pairs.
Across diverse computational and scientific domains, RSI-Anything demonstrates substantial gains. In code repair, with the coder fixed, success increases from 26.3%26.3\% with a static verifier to 29.2%29.2\% through predictive selection and 31.2%31.2\% after world-model adaptation, at comparable test usage. Graphical user interface (GUI) evaluations showed higher task completion, while average actions decrease by 39.4%39.4\%. Across five successive policy versions, laboratory-planning scores more than double from 21.80%21.80\% to 46.01%46.01\%. In reaction optimization, RSI-Anything achieves 96.15%96.15\% mean best observed yield versus 82.16%82.16\% for Bayesian optimization under the same 40-experiment budget. An end-to-end immune-science application further connects workflow evolution and predictive modeling with therapeutic hypothesis generation.
These results demonstrate how predictive world models and retained system updates enable recursive improvement across diverse environments.

1 Introduction

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) turns interaction experience into lasting improvements in a system’s ability to act and learn. Within tasks, feedback refines solutions or guides actions Cobbe et al. (2021); Shinn et al. (2023). Across rounds, self-training and preference optimization support persistent policy or procedural updates Gulcehre et al. (2023); Rafailov et al. (2023); Zelikman et al. (2022). Recursiveness requires these updates to be retained and shape subsequent interaction and learning. The central question is how experience from one round improves the system’s ability to act and learn in the next.

Learning from external feedback requires executing or evaluating candidates before their consequences can guide improvement, limiting exploration under constrained interaction budgets. World models predict the effects of candidate actions on subsequent states or outcomes Ha and Schmidhuber (2018); Hafner et al. (2025); Schrittwieser et al. (2020), enabling prospective evaluation and planning. Within RSI, these predictions can prioritize feedback collection and guide learning-data selection or construction. World models thus provide a predictive foundation for improvement under limited external feedback.

JEPA-Anything provides a shared framework for world-model construction across heterogeneous domains Cui et al. (2026b). Building on joint-embedding predictive architectures Assran et al. (2023); Bardes et al. (2024); Assran et al. (2025), it predicts target representations from observed context. Its orthogonal predictive factorization (OPF) decomposes latent targets into complementary factors, learns dedicated predictors, and recombines their outputs into a complete latent state Cui et al. (2026b). This provides a predictive foundation across domains (Figure 1). However, prediction alone does not establish recursive improvement: predicted outcomes must guide interaction, while external feedback must produce updates that persist across subsequent rounds.

Closing this loop introduces a coupled-update challenge. Policy updates change the candidate actions and experience distributions on which the world model operates, potentially undermining prediction reliability. Conversely, world-model updates alter candidate rankings, imagined trajectories, and the learning signals guiding policy improvement. Consequently, improving either component independently does not guarantee better joint performance. Reliable RSI therefore requires determining when to update each component, assessing prediction reliability under changing policies, and ensuring that retained policy–model pairs improve subsequent interaction and learning.

To address these challenges, we introduce RSI-Anything, a domain-general framework that integrates predictive world models with feedback-driven recursive improvement (Figure 2). World-model predictions guide action selection, prioritize external evaluations, and support learning-data selection or construction. External feedback drives policy learning or workflow revision and triggers world-model adaptation when reliability checks warrant it. The policy and world model can evolve asynchronously, with component-level checks evaluating individual updates and compatibility checks determining which policy–model pair is retained. This retained pair shapes subsequent candidates and learning experience, closing the recursive improvement loop.

Our evaluations examine whether introducing world-model predictions into RSI improves candidate selection, whether retained policy or model updates yield further improvements, and whether these gains can be achieved under limited external feedback. We investigate these questions across general computational tasks (code repair and GUI interaction), physical-domain planning, and experimental science. We further present an end-to-end application of RSI-Anything in immune science, spanning scientific-agent workflow evolution, immune world-model construction, analysis with self-correction, and prospective therapeutic hypothesis generation Cui et al. (2026a).

Our contributions are threefold:

• 

World models for recursive self-improvement. We introduce RSI-Anything, a domain-general framework that integrates predictive world models into recursive self-improvement to anticipate action outcomes and guide experience collection. We formulate persistent improvement at the level of the policy–world-model system, making prediction reliability under evolving policies a central design requirement.

• 

A recursive update-and-retention mechanism. We connect prediction-guided action and learning-data selection with feedback-driven policy learning or workflow revision and conditional world-model adaptation. Component and compatibility checks determine which policy–model pair is retained, allowing asynchronous updates. The retained pair shapes subsequent candidates and learning experience, linking each accepted system revision to the next round of improvement.

• 

Empirical evidence across domains. With the coder fixed, we demonstrate successive gains from introducing world-model-guided selection and adapting the predictor at similar online test usage. Further evaluations show higher GUI task completion, improvement across five successive laboratory-planning policy versions, and higher reaction yields than Bayesian optimization at matched experiment counts. An end-to-end immune-science application illustrates how RSI-Anything connects retained workflows and predictive models with evidence-guided analysis and hypothesis generation.

2 RSI-Anything Framework

RSI-Anything uses world-model predictions to guide action selection and feedback collection, then uses the resulting experience to propose and retain system updates (Figure 2). The retained policy–model pair determines subsequent candidates and the experience available for further improvement. The shared mechanism connects prediction-guided selection, feedback-driven updates, and component and pair acceptance. Domain implementations specify the models, action encodings, outcome readouts, and enabled pathways, including uncertainty-aware selection, multistep planning, and imagined-trajectory learning. The full update-and-retention protocol is defined below; Table 4 identifies the update scope reported for each application.

2.4 Feedback-driven improvement

External feedback extends history ℋk\mathcal{H}_{k} with prediction–observation pairs linked to the generating system version. Records selected for policy improvement form 𝒟policy,k\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{policy},k}; records selected for predictor adaptation form 𝒟adapt,k\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{adapt},k}. The two sets serve different objectives and need not contain the same records. Policy improvement changes how candidates are generated or selected; predictor adaptation changes how their consequences are forecast.

Policy learning or workflow revision.

Feedback must be converted into a signal appropriate to the update object. Demonstration-based learning uses selected actions or trajectories as targets; preference learning compares alternatives using their evaluated outcomes; workflow search uses evaluation feedback to rank persistent agent-workflow configurations. Each application selects the update route appropriate to its policy representation and feedback. Eligible imagined trajectories can supplement policy data when that route is enabled. The policy update seeks better task behavior under the declared objective and constraints.

Let ukπu_{k}^{\pi} indicate an enabled policy-update attempt at its declared schedule or feedback trigger, with the required learning evidence and sufficient budget for the proposed attempt. A fixed-policy setting has ukπ=0u_{k}^{\pi}=0:

The instantiated route specifies how feedback constructs training targets or workflow scores, which parameters or configuration fields can change, and when updates are attempted.

Conditional world-model adaptation.

Adaptation uses observed targets to improve the corresponding predictions. Outcome labels can supervise an outcome readout; latent prediction targets require an observation that the domain target encoder can represent. A scalar outcome supplies an outcome target rather than a fully observed target state, although its loss can update upstream parameters when they are trainable. The adaptation specification identifies the available target, its predictive loss, and the trainable parameter subset. These choices can differ from those used in initial model construction.

The reliability protocol requires predictions attributable to the current model version. For each historical observation included in the assessment, it uses either a stored prediction generated by that version or a new prediction obtained by applying the current model to the recorded input. Earlier-version predictions remain available for analysis of past decisions but are not pooled directly into the current error estimate. The implementation specifies record selection, version handling, and whether evaluation records have also been used for training.

2.5 Recursive retention

The full retention protocol compares proposed components and their assembled pair with the current versions under predeclared acceptance criteria. Component checks address policy task utility and constraints, or predictor quality on relevant targets. The pair check addresses the dependency between them: a policy can generate candidates for which an otherwise improved predictor is unreliable, and a predictor can change which candidates are selected. Pair acceptance therefore concerns the decision behavior of the assembled system and predictor suitability on candidates proposed by the selected policy, beyond isolated component quality.

The evaluation specification fixes the task metrics, prediction checks, constraints, feedback allowance, and acceptance thresholds. Corresponding task conditions can be matched even when the two policies generate different candidates. Failure of a component check retains its current version; failure of the pair check retains the entire current pair. If neither component changes, the current pair carries forward without a new pair comparison. Updates are retained when the candidate pair meets the prespecified acceptance criteria under matched evaluation conditions.

Update data construct candidate versions, gate data select which versions are retained, and any designated held-out audit set is reserved for final reporting.

The retained pair and accumulated feedback history enter the next cycle. A changed policy can alter which candidates are proposed; a changed predictor can alter rankings and learning signals. Together, these changes affect which actions receive feedback and what experience is available for subsequent updates. This dependence of later interaction and learning on retained revisions defines the recursive mechanism. The six operations in Figure 2 therefore connect candidate proposal and prediction to external selection, observation, update construction, and system retention.

3 Results

We examined how world-model predictions improve action selection, how retained updates support further improvement, and how prediction and feedback guide decisions under limited interaction budgets. Code repair and GUI interaction cover general computational tasks, alongside physical-domain laboratory planning and experimental reaction optimization. Within these settings, we evaluate predictive selection, model adaptation, task completion, successive policy versions, and search at matched experiment counts. We then examine an end-to-end application of RSI-Anything in immune science, connecting scientific-agent workflow evolution, world-model construction, and scientific analysis.

3.1 World-model-guided selection and adaptation improve code repair

We evaluated two contributions of RSI-Anything in code repair: introducing world-model predictions for candidate selection and obtaining further gains by updating the predictor with execution feedback. The ReasonFlux-Coder-7B policy was held fixed Wang et al. (2025). We compared RSI-Anything with a fixed world model (CURE-WM-Lite) and RSI-Anything with world-model adaptation. All selection methods used the same coder and candidate budget: four revisions were proposed before one was selected for execution. Evaluation covered 200 CodeContests tasks Li et al. (2022) across three seeds and up to three feedback rounds, with online tests supplying feedback and hidden tests measuring final success.

4 Related Work

4.1 Recursive self-improvement

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) has evolved from improving reasoning and code to adapting interactive agents and their underlying systems. In coding, CURE co-evolves code generation and unit testing through execution feedback Wang et al. (2025), while Self-Improving Coding Agent and Darwin Godel Machine iteratively modify agent implementations and retain empirically validated improvements Robeyns et al. (2025); Zhang et al. (2025). AIDE2 extends this approach to self-improving AI research agents Srikanth et al. (2026).
Beyond code, Voyager accumulates reusable executable skills Wang et al. (2023), while GenEnv and Agent0 adapt task curricula to evolving agent capabilities Guo et al. (2025); Xia et al. (2025). RLAnything jointly optimizes the policy, reward model, and environment Wang et al. (2026b), and OpenClaw-RL enables online policy improvement from user interactions and tool feedback Wang et al. (2026a).
Long-horizon RSI further requires improvements to persist across extended interactions. Recuris couples Experiential and Working Memory with validation-gated memory evolution Yu et al. (2026), while ScienceBuddy introduces nested self-improvement through harness refinement and model reinforcement learning Xue et al. (2026). These approaches improve agent code, policies, memories, harnesses, or training environments. RSI-Anything extends this line by explicitly incorporating predictive world models into the recursive loop: predictions guide candidate selection, observations assess model reliability, and policy–model compatibility determines which updated components are retained for subsequent interactions.

4.2 Verification and policy self-improvement

Verifiers, self-training, preference optimization, and verbal feedback turn evaluated trajectories into signals for selection and learning Cobbe et al. (2021); Zelikman et al. (2022); Rafailov et al. (2023); Gulcehre et al. (2023); Shinn et al. (2023). CURE Wang et al. (2025) co-evolves a coder and unit tester, providing the ReasonFlux-Coder policy used in our code evaluation. RLAnything jointly optimizes the policy, reward model, and environment through feedback Wang et al. (2026b). These approaches motivate the feedback-to-policy part of RSI-Anything. Our framework additionally makes world-model reliability and policy–model compatibility explicit in update acceptance: changing the policy changes the candidate distribution on which its predictor must be evaluated.

4.3 Predictive world models

World Models Ha and Schmidhuber (2018), MuZero Schrittwieser et al. (2020), Dreamer Hafner et al. (2025), and Genie Bruce et al. (2024) establish predictive environments as a basis for control, planning, and interactive generation. Joint-embedding predictive architectures learn latent targets without reconstructing every observation detail Assran et al. (2023); Bardes et al. (2024); Assran et al. (2025), and JEPA-Anything extends this line through OPF across heterogeneous data types Cui et al. (2026b). Building on these predictive capabilities, RSI-Anything organizes how predictions and observed outcomes guide action selection and the acceptance of subsequent policy or world-model versions.

4.4 Scientific and laboratory agents

Vision-language-action and cross-embodiment datasets motivate action-conditioned models for physical systems Brohan et al. (2023); Open X-Embodiment Collaboration et al. (2023); Khazatsky et al. (2024). LabHorizon measures visual next-action prediction and protocol-aligned planning Stanford Cong Lab (2026). In chemistry, language-model tools, yield predictors, Bayesian optimization, and robotic laboratories provide complementary components for selecting and testing reactions Boiko et al. (2023); Bran et al. (2024); Schwaller et al. (2021); Shields et al. (2021); Burger et al. (2020); Szymanski et al. (2023). ReactionT5 Sagawa and Kojima (2025) is a reaction-pretrained transformer that supports product, retrosynthesis, and yield prediction; our chemistry adapter combines this reaction-prediction backbone with the predictive design of JEPA-Anything Cui et al. (2026b) for action-conditioned yield forecasting. Single-cell foundation models and perturbation predictors support cellular-response forecasting Cui et al. (2024); Theodoris et al. (2023); Roohani et al. (2024); Bunne et al. (2023). RSI-Anything connects these domain capabilities through a common transition and feedback contract, so that the outcome of a program, experiment, or intervention can inform candidate selection and subsequent system updates.

5 Discussion

The results support two roles for world models in recursive improvement: informing which actions receive external feedback and providing a predictor that can itself improve from that feedback. Predictive selection and model adaptation yielded successive gains in code repair, while retained policy versions improved laboratory planning. In GUI tasks, RSI-Anything updated both the policy and the world model and achieved higher task completion, with fewer repeated clicks and incorrect-submission episodes on the checkbox task. Together with measured-feedback search and the end-to-end immune-science workflow, these findings connect prediction-guided decisions to learning and retained system changes. The common contribution is a procedure for using prediction–observation pairs to guide current actions and assess changes to the system that will make subsequent decisions.

The design links decisions about actions to decisions about system versions. Pairing predictions with observations allows the same experience to inform candidate utility and predictive reliability. Policies and world models can change on different schedules: a reliable predictor can support policy or workflow development, and new outcome evidence can motivate predictor adaptation while the policy remains fixed. Reliability checks govern when model adaptation is attempted, and component and compatibility checks govern which version is carried forward. Retaining accepted versions makes subsequent experience depend on the system’s accumulated improvements.

Domain generality concerns the reuse of this improvement mechanism across domain-specific world models, policies, and evaluation procedures. The evaluations support predictive selection, predictor adaptation, successive policy improvement, and feedback-guided search in their respective settings. Joint multiround evaluation is needed to establish sustained mutual improvement of the policy and world model within a single task. The present laboratory results assess protocol-aligned planning; reliable physical execution requires evaluation on the relevant equipment and workflows. The chemistry results cover two Suzuki–Miyaura screens under the reported experiment budgets, and the therapeutic hypothesis generated in the immune-science application awaits experimental testing.

6.1 Domain-specific evaluation settings

Code repair.

The state contains the current program, generated tests, and observed feedback; each action is a candidate revision. The ReasonFlux-Coder-7B policy proposes four revisions and is held fixed Wang et al. (2025). W1 is our 3B execution-outcome world model, built using the predictive design of JEPA-Anything Cui et al. (2026b) and adapted from Qwen2.5-Coder-3B-Instruct Hui et al. (2024) with LoRA Hu et al. (2022). For every candidate–test pair, it returns the probability of each execution-result class. Both RSI-Anything configurations aggregate these probabilities to rank the four revisions before one revision is executed. The fixed-world-model configuration (CURE-WM-Lite) retains W1, whereas the world-model-adaptation configuration updates W1 with the coder held fixed. We evaluated 200 CodeContests tasks Li et al. (2022) across three seeds and at most three feedback rounds. The comparison included both RSI-Anything configurations, random selection, and a capacity-matched static verifier. Metrics were final hidden-test all-pass rate, online test calls per task, candidate-ranking accuracy, and five-class Brier score. Online tests supply feedback during selection; hidden tests supply the final reported success measure.

We additionally evaluated APPS Introductory (1,000 problems) and LiveCodeBench v6 (1,055 problems) using seeds 0, 1, and 2. Repair methods shared the initial program for each problem and seed and used the same ReasonFlux-Coder-7B policy, with four repair candidates per round and at most three repair rounds. Public-test feedback guided repair, which stopped when all public tests passed. Final success required passing the complete official test suite.

GUI agents.

We evaluated the MiniWoB++ click-checkboxes task Liu et al. (2018) and OSWorld Xie et al. (2024). World-model predictions guide GUI action selection, and observed interaction outcomes provide feedback for updating both the action policy and the world model. The retained policy–model pair is used in subsequent interactions. The checkbox evaluation compares baseline, adapted, and final configurations using task success rate, mean actions per episode, repeated-click count, and the number of episodes with incorrect submissions. Success rates are also shown for the World-based control. The action and error diagnostics in Figure 4e–g describe the RSI-Anything configurations. The OSWorld comparison includes the initial agent, RLAnything Wang et al. (2026b), and RSI-Anything. Figure 4d uses the initial agent as the common reference; the additional RSI-Anything comparison uses RLAnything as its reference.

Laboratory planning.

LabHorizon evaluates Level-1 next-action accuracy and Level-2 protocol-aligned action sequences on its 200-task F200 setting Stanford Cong Lab (2026). Level-2 Final Score combines action-sequence similarity and parameter accuracy. The RSI-Anything policy versions use a 4B model to generate plans and a JEPA-Anything-based protocol world model to predict their consequences. The F200 evaluation follows five consecutive 4B policy versions from the same RSI-Anything self-improvement process, ranging from 0.21800.2180 to 0.46010.4601. Reaction optimization.

We construct the ReactionT5–JEPA-Anything adapter by combining the ReactionT5 reaction-prediction backbone Sagawa and Kojima (2025) with the predictive design of JEPA-Anything Cui et al. (2026b). It maps a serialized reaction rr and candidate condition vector cc to a predicted yield distribution pϕ​(y∣r,c)p_{\phi}(y\mid r,c). Results are reported as best observed or mean best observed yield for each separate protocol. Measured yields guide subsequent condition choices, and performance is summarized by the best observed yield at matched experiment counts.

End-to-end immune-science application.

The immune-science application uses an action-conditioned immune world model that encodes cellular state, tissue immune context, and individual context, and treats cytokines, genetic perturbations, or candidate therapies as explicit actions Cui et al. (2026a). Agent Genesis evolved scientific-agent workflows and model configurations, producing a model for withheld-intervention forecasting, cross-scale prediction, and action composition. The selected model-building agent workflow is the retained policy configuration in this construction stage. During downstream intervention analysis, the resulting world-model parameters are frozen while the analysis state, candidate interventions, and hypothesis ranking evolve through model inference and self-correction. Therapeutic-hypothesis generation combined measured perturbation evidence with this model-guided analysis. The resulting prospective nomination combines SIRPα\alpha inhibition with IL-36γ\gamma-axis modulation for subsequent experimental testing.

6.2 Statistical reporting

Success rates are reported over 600 CodeContests task-seed evaluations. We report the paired percentage-point difference between RSI-Anything with a fixed world model and the static verifier, together with its 95% interval. APPS and LiveCodeBench report the arithmetic mean of final full-test success rates across three seeds, corresponding to 3,000 and 3,165 problem–seed evaluations, respectively. LabHorizon F200 reports mean scores over 200 tasks Stanford Cong Lab (2026). The 24.2124.21- and 0.690.69-percentage-point gains are differences between these benchmark means.
